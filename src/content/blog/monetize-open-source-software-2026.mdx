Thousands of brilliant engineers build open-source tools with thousands of GitHub stars, only to burn out handling bug reports for free while billion-dollar enterprises profit off their sweat equity.

Building in public does not require working for charity. Today, the most resilient developer-first companies (Supabase, PostHog, Cal.com, Docker) run successful commercial open-source software (COSS) companies.

Here are the 6 battle-tested business models to turn your repository into a sustainable business.

---

## 1. Managed Cloud Hosting (The SaaS Model)

The single highest-margin open-source business model is offering a **1-click managed cloud** of your open-source software.

* **The Value Proposition**: Developers love having access to the code, but enterprises despise maintaining Linux servers, configuring automated off-site backups, and handling SSL rotation.
* **Real-World Examples**: PostHog, Plausible Analytics, Ghost, Supabase.
* **Pricing**: A generous free tier for developers, followed by $20–$250+/month usage-based tiers for teams.

---

## 2. Open Core (Community vs Enterprise Features)

Under the Open Core model, the foundational core engine is 100% free under a permissive license (MIT, Apache 2.0), while advanced enterprise features are proprietary.

### Features That Enterprises Will Happily Pay For:
* **SAML / SSO (Single Sign-On)** via Okta or Azure AD.
* **Audit Logs & Compliance Reports** (SOC2, HIPAA, GDPR).
* **Role-Based Access Control (RBAC)** and fine-grained team permissions.
* **Multi-tenant clustering & custom SLAs**.

---

## 3. Paid Developer Extensions & Themes

If your tool has an ecosystem (like an IDE, CMS, or CLI), you can create a marketplace of premium extensions, plugins, or UI themes.

* **Examples**: Tailwind UI (made by the creators of Tailwind CSS, earning millions annually without altering the free framework license), Astro Themes, Obsidian Sync.

---

## 4. Dual Licensing (AGPLv3 to Commercial)

By licensing your software under the **GNU Affero General Public License (AGPLv3)**, any company that modifies your software and runs it as a network service must make their modifications public.

Companies that wish to embed your software into their closed-source commercial products must purchase a **Commercial Proprietary License** from you.

---

## 5. Paid Technical Support & Consultation Retainers

For critical backend infrastructure, companies will pay **$2,000 to $10,000/month** simply to have your direct Slack access and guaranteed 2-hour SLA support in case of production outages.

---

## Key Takeaway: Stars Don't Pay the Bills

GitHub stars are vanity metrics; cash flow is sanity. If you build software that saves an engineer 5 hours a week or saves an enterprise $10,000 in operational downtime, do not be afraid to charge what it is worth.
